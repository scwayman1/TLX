# GemOne official product and customer-material research stream

**Scope.** Public GemOne/GEM One materials only; no authentication, form submission, control bypass, or non-public data. Retrieval completed 2026-08-22. “Date” below is the page modified date exposed in official page metadata where available; for PDFs, it is the year/month encoded in the official upload URL unless the document itself states a year.

## Executive readout

### Verified facts

- GemOne currently presents four named portfolio elements: **Sapphire** for material-handling fleet/safety management, **Onyx** for mixed rental/construction fleet management, **GemOne Cloud** as the consolidated fleet-data platform, and an **AI pedestrian-proximity camera**; its portfolio framing spans track-and-trace, security/safety, operational efficiency, service/maintenance, and sustainability.[1][13]
- **Sapphire** works across makes/models and combines mandatory pre-start checklists, critical-issue lockout, PIN/RFID access control, certification expiry lockout, impact reporting, hour-meter/utilization and session reporting. Optional add-ons include HD/AI cameras, load sensors, battery monitoring, and proximity warning.[2][14]
- Sapphire hardware claims include a 7-inch Android color display, IP67 protection, Wi-Fi/4G choices, global coverage, and approximately one-hour installation; GemOne Cloud provides reports, alerts, dashboards, and access from any device.[2][14]
- **Onyx** provides GPS/location history, geofencing/theft alerts, utilization and billable-hour insights, maintenance forecasting, remote CAN-data diagnostics, access management, and integration with ERP, invoicing, rental-planning, and service systems.[3][15]
- The current **Onyx V4** claims dual-CAN, 4G LTE with 2G fallback, built-in relays for ignition blocking/power switching, IP67 protection, and operation to 97 VDC with over-voltage protection to 120 V. The official installation guide further specifies GPS/GLONASS/BDS/Galileo/QZSS, 2.5 m stated receiver accuracy, Bluetooth 5.4/LE, eight analog/digital inputs, two CAN-FD buses, and two relay outputs.[15][24]
- A separate **battery-powered tracker** targets trailers, containers, bins, and other unpowered assets; GemOne claims up to ten years’ battery life at one location update per day, IP69K, GNSS plus Wi-Fi geolocation, NB-IoT, BLE 5.0, OTA firmware updates, remote update-rate control, and virtual tamper detection.[19]
- **GemOne Cloud** normalizes data from multiple sources into one platform and is positioned for remote maintenance, utilization/operator analytics, safety/compliance workflows, custom alerts/dashboards, report export/scheduling, and connection of trackers from other telematics vendors and existing business systems.[4][13][16]
- The **AI camera** claims audible/visual pedestrian warnings, 1080p resolution, IP69K, a 130° view, a single 25-foot detection zone, low-light recording, local playback, up to four cameras on one display, and up to 11 days of high-resolution SD-card video subject to camera count and shifts.[5][25]
- The **Proximity Warning Solution** is a 360° UWB system using forklift beacons and worker wearables, with audible/vibration/LED alerts, configurable range, cloud reporting, and standalone or GemOne-integrated operation. The brochure states CE/FCC/RoHS, 20 cm UWB accuracy, and IP ratings of IP64 for the wearable and IP67 for the beacon.[17]
- The **Battery Monitoring Solution** works standalone or with GemOne telematics and reports voltage, current, temperature, internal resistance, state of charge, and state of health. Its brochure specifies 8–95 V / 50–150 V variants, ±300 A / ±800 A measurement, 4G, BLE, RS485 MODBUS, and a level sensor.[18]
- GemOne publishes unusually broad public enablement material: Sapphire portal, camera, hardware, scale, troubleshooting and spec guides; Onyx hardware/access-control/portal guides; and add-on guides/spec sheets for proximity warning, battery monitoring, battery trackers, cameras, generators, and RFID compatibility.[11][12]
- The Sapphire portal guide independently confirms a substantial administrative/reporting surface: site and driver dashboards, utilization comparisons, checklist summaries/exceptions, expiring licenses, training scheduler, impact reports, session/hour reports, operator lists, equipment-access matrices, lockout thresholds, notification alerts, and national reporting.[23]

### Customer evidence

- **Balloo Hire / Onyx:** the official case study says Balloo began with 50 machines and grew to more than 1,200 connected assets across its mixed rental fleet; GemOne connected multiple makes/models and pre-existing third-party trackers, integrated with Balloo’s ERP, and provided real-time location, operation, maintenance and utilization visibility. These are vendor-published customer claims, not independently audited outcome measures.[22]
- **Atlas Toyota Material Handling / Sapphire:** the official article and PDF describe cloud-based data export/scheduled reporting, checklist-driven safety compliance, impact analysis/lockout, PIN/RFID access, license-expiry lockout, session and hour-meter reporting, and proactive service scheduling across customer sites. The source includes testimonials and qualitative benefits but no controlled before/after ROI dataset.[20][21]
- Atlas reported that manual key-hour collection could consume **3–4 technician hours** per visit before Sapphire; the case study later describes pulling 11 locations into one report and using real-time hour data to schedule service. Treat the 3–4 hour figure as a customer anecdote, not a generalized benchmark.[21]

### Interpretations

- **Portfolio architecture:** GemOne appears to be converging hardware, add-ons, and mixed-vendor telemetry onto GemOne Cloud while segmenting the primary application layer by buyer/use case: Sapphire for material handling and Onyx for rental/construction. This is an interpretation of the official product and product-range materials, not an explicit packaging statement.[1][13]
- **Competitive emphasis:** the strongest differentiators in official materials are mixed-fleet/mixed-vendor aggregation, industrial hardware breadth, safety workflow depth, and public implementation documentation. Claims of “single source of truth,” reduced incidents, lower costs, or improved productivity remain vendor value propositions unless paired with quantified customer evidence.[4][13][20]
- **Go-to-market motion:** public materials support both fleet owners and channel/dealer users, including dealer access to hour data, technical training, post-sales account management, and customer-facing analytics. This suggests a direct-plus-channel model, but contract structure and partner economics are not public.[10][14][21]

## Evidence gaps and diligence questions

1. **Commercials:** no public list pricing, subscription tiers, minimum terms, hardware financing, warranty exclusions, implementation fees, or partner margins were found in the reviewed official materials.
2. **Cloud architecture/security:** no public SLA, uptime history, tenancy model, hosting regions, retention rules, disaster-recovery targets, SOC 2/ISO 27001 attestation, penetration-test summary, SSO/SAML/SCIM detail, role-permission model, or encryption/key-management specification was found. The site makes a general GDPR-compliance claim for tracking data, but the reviewed materials do not substantiate the control framework.[6]
3. **Integration depth:** official pages claim connection to ERP, invoicing, rental planning, service applications, and third-party trackers, but do not publish API docs, webhook/event schemas, supported vendors, rate limits, write-back behavior, or implementation boundaries.[3][4][10]
4. **Performance validation:** no public independent studies were found for GPS accuracy in deployed conditions, AI-camera pedestrian detection precision/recall, false alarms, latency, UWB performance under obstruction, impact-sensor calibration, or load-sensor accuracy across installations. The load-sensor brochure claim of accuracy within 2% of rated capacity should be validated per machine/configuration.[14]
5. **Quantified outcomes:** customer stories are persuasive but mostly qualitative. Balloo provides adoption scale, while Atlas provides workflow anecdotes; neither supplies a verified ROI model, incident-rate reduction, downtime percentage, utilization lift, battery-life improvement, or support-response KPI.[21][22]
6. **Product/version clarity:** the public index mixes current and old collateral, region-specific variants, and legacy Sapphire V2/V2S/V3 and Onyx OX-G/OX-L/OX-C materials. Buyers should confirm current SKUs, backward compatibility, migration paths, feature parity by region/connectivity, and support/EOL dates.[11][12]
7. **Safety positioning:** several materials use OSHA-compliance language, but telematics functionality should not be treated as legal certification or a substitute for site-specific risk assessment, training, inspection, and regulatory diligence.[2][8][21]

## Research handling notes

- Direct brochure aliases were followed only through ordinary public HTTP redirects to the official `gemone.com/wp-content/uploads/` PDFs.
- No lead-generation form was submitted; where a current page required email delivery, the public brochures/install indexes supplied direct official files.
- Several legacy `/en/...` URLs in the old sitemap now return 404 or redirect to language-neutral current pages. The source register therefore uses current canonical URLs and notes old collateral where relevant.
